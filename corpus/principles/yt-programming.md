# Programming, fatigue and deloads (Jeff Nippard + Renaissance Periodization)

Sources: Nippard LT_aBQatj5s; RP Dt6ok24VBpg, 3lrxQ3nHOmA, 2e6N9nITAYg, -8Sgczn4VsM, i69D6PW785s, 9d7NI_entvg, bZod37igzQk (guest sports scientist with Dr. Mike), BXLu46kBeXM (James Hoffmann). ## Why deload: fitness vs fatigue
- Training raises fitness and fatigue together; when both are high, performance suffers. Max on Monday and again Tuesday and you are weaker Tuesday because fatigue masks the gain (Nippard, LT_aBQatj5s @ 0:58).
- Fatigue compounds. If recovery between weeks is 90%, you carry 81%, 72%, 63% and are near half after five weeks (RP, 2e6N9nITAYg @ 7:59).
- Fatigue degrades abilities in this order: speed, power, strength, size, endurance. Strength needs low-to-moderate fatigue, power low, speed very low; size tolerates a lot (RP, 2e6N9nITAYg @ 10:05).
- Excess fatigue also blunts adaptation at the cell level and raises injury and illness risk (RP, 2e6N9nITAYg @ 12:08).
- Overload outranks fatigue management. Making training easy all the time does not work; zero fatigue is not the goal (RP, 2e6N9nITAYg @ 17:38, 21:06).
- A deload serves two goals: dump accumulated fatigue (usually after 4-8 weeks) and keep the adaptations (RP, Dt6ok24VBpg @ 0:43).

## When to deload
- Proactive schedule: every 4-8 weeks, more often the more advanced you are, because advanced lifters train harder and make more fatigue (Nippard, LT_aBQatj5s @ 4:56).
- Reactive: deload when you cannot improve or maintain. Nippard distrusts this alone because tendons and joints are poorly innervated and you can accumulate joint stress unnoticed (Nippard, LT_aBQatj5s @ 5:12-5:53; RP, 2e6N9nITAYg @ 26:29).
- RP says use both. Autoregulate for glycogen and nervous-system fatigue (you feel it fast). Keep planned deloads even when you feel great, because tissue damage is imperceptible (RP, 2e6N9nITAYg @ 24:34-27:46).
- Two-session confirmation: a single bad session may be noise. Performance flat or down for two sessions in a row (not necessarily two weeks) means deload (RP, Dt6ok24VBpg @ 6:50). Retry the same pattern or a different muscle later in the week; a second miss confirms it (RP, 3lrxQ3nHOmA @ 8:19, 9:13).
- Any drop where you tried your hardest is almost always excess fatigue. Example: 315x10 then 320x10 is fine; 325 for 10,8,7,7,6 is not (RP, 3lrxQ3nHOmA @ 8:40-9:35).
- Stress counts. Psychological stress adds to the same pool, so an unexplained two-session dip warrants a deload regardless of source (RP, Dt6ok24VBpg @ 7:29).
- If you are near your maximum recoverable volume (for example 19 sets of biceps held, then performance falls), back off (RP, Dt6ok24VBpg @ 8:17).
- A deload that is too hard shows up later. Fatigue is exponential, so week 1 feels fine, week 3 collapses, and you need an unplanned mid-cycle deload (RP, Dt6ok24VBpg @ 21:07).

## Who needs one (training age)
- Beginner is defined by progress, not years: you gain from partial adherence. Intermediate begins at your first real plateau. Advanced means nothing progresses unless the details align and meets go up and down. Each lift can sit in a different tier (RP, -8Sgczn4VsM @ 0:13-2:21).
- Early beginners (first year) should not generate enough fatigue to need a formal deload. Late beginner to early intermediate (1-2 years) is where deloads start to make sense (Nippard, LT_aBQatj5s @ 6:00).
- RP gives different cutoffs: novices of six months or less, and "beginners" of three years or less, can mostly ignore fatigue management (RP, 2e6N9nITAYg @ 28:10). Nippard's year and RP's progress-based definition disagree on timing; progress-based is more robust [inference].
- "Lazy lifters" who never train hard are effectively deloading every week and should fix consistency first (Nippard, LT_aBQatj5s @ 6:16).
- Beginners: sets of 5-10, mostly compound basics and few exercises, whole-body sessions 3-4x/week, avoid RPE 10 and near-maximum volume, add load only when technique is stable (RP, -8Sgczn4VsM @ 2:21-4:43).
- Intermediates: 5-10 reps still, plus 3-6. Example cycle: three months of 5-10, two months of 3-6, back to 5-10. Each movement 2-4x/week in 4-6 sessions. Mesocycles push RPE 7 to 10. Late-intermediate volume often stays constant (for example four sets throughout) and only load rises, since load alone takes you from minimum effective to maximum recoverable volume (RP, -8Sgczn4VsM @ 4:51-8:52).
- Advanced: mostly 3-6, RPE 8-9 with RPE 10 rare (injury risk, big fatigue toll), each pattern 2-3x/week, set progression is usually nil and only load rises, sometimes sets fall as load rises (RP, -8Sgczn4VsM @ 9:25-13:07).

## How to deload
- Rule of thumb: when you hit the brakes, hit them hard. A deload should feel like "meh". If it impresses people or you leave with a big pump or a PR, it is not a deload (RP, Dt6ok24VBpg @ 4:39-5:13, 20:34).
- Nippard standard deload: cut volume 30-50% (one or two sets per exercise: 3-4 sets becomes 2-3), drop intensity modestly (RPE 8-10 becomes 6-8), and use the week for technique and mind-muscle connection. Instinctive version: a light week, a few more reps from failure, one less set (Nippard, LT_aBQatj5s @ 3:36-4:47).
- Nippard taper (before a max test): about half the sets, accessories cut, loads stay heavy. Example: 8 weekly squat sets, then 4 the week before the test (Nippard, LT_aBQatj5s @ 2:48-3:34).
- RP hypertrophy method 1: first half of the week sets x0.5, reps x0.5, load unchanged (heavy load conserves muscle). Second half same sets and reps at half load. Best for beginner/early-intermediate joints (RP, Dt6ok24VBpg @ 9:13-10:51).
- RP hypertrophy method 2: first half keep sets and reps, load x0.5 (for example 6x10 at 150 instead of 300), second half cut sets. Spares joints more, so it suits big, strong lifters whose joints dislike heavy loads (RP, Dt6ok24VBpg @ 11:00-13:20).
- RP strength method 1: load -10% and sets x0.5 early in the week (6x5 at 400 becomes 3x5 at 360), then sets x0.5 and load x0.5 later. Method 2 for advanced: sets x0.5 and load x0.5 all week (RP, Dt6ok24VBpg @ 15:05-17:39).
- Full week off: acceptable for hypertrophy (RP, Dt6ok24VBpg @ 14:10). Not ideal for strength because technique degrades; use a very light week and practice technique (RP, Dt6ok24VBpg @ 17:47). Nippard prefers a modified week over zero training: weights feel heavy after a full week off. A week off is fine on vacation or for a mental break (Nippard, LT_aBQatj5s @ 2:01-2:48).
- Research: in trained lifters, a week off every four weeks gave the same hypertrophy as nine continuous weeks. There is no direct trial of light training vs week off, but experienced coaches (Olympic lifting, IFBB, powerlifting) all recommend some light work. Activity beats rest for recovery in team-sport studies (bZod37igzQk @ 24:49-26:05).
- During a deload, no overloading training of any kind, including new activities or lighter-but-higher-rep strength work (bZod37igzQk @ 27:52-28:44).
- Success check: by the last day you should feel fresher, sharper and eager to train. If the next block starts and you are not near 100%, the deload was too hard (RP, Dt6ok24VBpg @ 20:01-20:34).
- If the last accumulation week was RPE 10 and you were gassed, the cycle length and volume were right. If you finished fresh, add sets next cycle or lengthen it by a week (RP, 9d7NI_entvg @ 4:44-5:25).

## Fatigue indicators (leading, concurrent, lagging)
- Leading (red lights that fatigue is coming, never one-for-one): several days of poor nutrition or too few calories; several days of stress or poor sleep; a very hard previous week (RP, 3lrxQ3nHOmA @ 2:56-4:58).
- Technique and coordination falling off is among the first signs, ahead of strength by roughly a week or two (RP, 3lrxQ3nHOmA @ 5:06; 2e6N9nITAYg @ 8:53).
- Jump height cresting and falling is a leading indicator for strength/size lifters and a concurrent one for explosive athletes (RP, 3lrxQ3nHOmA @ 5:54).
- Concurrent: slower bar speed, weights feeling heavier than normal (about half the time it is nothing), and above all performance underruns (RP, 3lrxQ3nHOmA @ 7:06-9:42). Grip dynamometer drops can help when performance is hard to measure (RP, 3lrxQ3nHOmA @ 9:42).
- Lagging ("now, today or tomorrow"): HRV or heart rate off (weak alone, useful as one extra measure), lost desire to train in a lifter who normally loves it, mood disturbance, appetite suppression on foods you normally eat, poor sleep or early waking, illness, wear-and-tear injuries, and a bad competition (RP, 3lrxQ3nHOmA @ 10:21-17:05).
- Illness often arrives as you ease off (sympathetic drop). If sick while training harder and harder, stop and deload (RP, 3lrxQ3nHOmA @ 14:53-16:10).
- If many lagging signs show midweek, start the deload the next day rather than grinding to the planned date (RP, 3lrxQ3nHOmA @ 17:27-18:16).
- Performance in the gym is still the main thing; if you are hitting PRs you are fine (RP, 3lrxQ3nHOmA @ 18:24).

## Progression (strength)
- Start with a load in the target rep range at about RPE 7 in week 1. Plan for maximum recoverable volume at the last week (for example week 5 of 5). Log every rep, set and load (RP, 9d7NI_entvg @ 0:14-2:08).
- Ranges: 5-10 reps for strength-hypertrophy, 3-6 basic strength, 1-3 peaking (RP, 9d7NI_entvg @ 0:22).
- Progress by adding load, not reps. Add just enough weight so RPE stays the same or rises by one (7 to 7 or 8, 8 to 8 or 9), never a jump like 7 to 9 (RP, 9d7NI_entvg @ 2:16-3:15).
- Performance must beat last week every session. If you need RPE 10 to beat an RPE 7 week, something is wrong (RP, 9d7NI_entvg @ 3:15-3:40).
- Typical jumps: 5 lb per week, sometimes 2.5, deadlifts 10-15. Try less first; add in the next set if you undershot (RP, 9d7NI_entvg @ 4:12-4:36).
- Keep sets roughly constant across the cycle. When adding load stops working and reps fall off, deload and repeat (RP, 9d7NI_entvg @ 4:36, 5:41).
- Beginners: add load only when technique is stable or improving. If technique degrades in a session, repeat the same load next session (RP, -8Sgczn4VsM @ 3:33).
- Intermediates should not test constantly; most of the time should be training (RP, -8Sgczn4VsM @ 5:07).

## Stimulus-to-fatigue ratio (exercise choice)
- Goal for each hypertrophy exercise: maximum stimulus to the target muscle with minimum fatigue (joint and systemic). Highest stimulus with low fatigue is best; high stimulus with high fatigue is tolerable; low stimulus with high fatigue (quarter squats) is worst (RP, i69D6PW785s @ 2:03, 17:37-21:21).
- Stimulus proxies (score out of 3): mind-muscle connection (felt tension at 5-15 reps or burn at 15-30), pump from few sets, and muscle disruption (weakness, cramping, soreness). 3/3 is plenty, 2/3 fine, 1/3 doubtful, 0/3 do not expect growth (RP, i69D6PW785s @ 3:47-11:20).
- Fatigue proxies: joint and connective-tissue pain during or hours after (do not trust "feels fine" when warmed up), perceived exertion needed for the stimulus, and strength drop in unused muscles after the exercise (RP, i69D6PW785s @ 11:36-17:06).
- No universal list; test and note it. Tweak grip, stance, range of motion, bar path, cadence. Keep the 3-4 variants that work. Techniques go stale with long use, so rotate (RP, i69D6PW785s @ 22:01-29:19).
- Intermediates should explore many exercises to find the best SFR for them; advanced lifters stick to those (RP, -8Sgczn4VsM @ 6:04, 10:30).

## Recovery methods ranked
- S tier: sleep and food. Chronic short sleep ruins training; a hypocaloric state turns recovery down. Below roughly a 500 kcal deficit or about 1.5 g/kg protein, gains suffer (bZod37igzQk @ 28:51-31:41).
- Stress management is a major lever: avoid overloading life outside the gym and schedule real relaxation, not recovery chores. "What do I remove?" beats "what do I add?" (bZod37igzQk @ 31:58-38:56).
- A tier: deloads. They are the only modality that gives time back (bZod37igzQk @ 22:49-26:25, 28:44).
- Sauna: no effect on growth, fine if you like it, 15-30 minutes, not an extreme heat contest (bZod37igzQk @ 20:40-22:34).
- Massage and massage guns: relaxation (parasympathetic) is the main benefit; no strong growth evidence; keep for injuries or enjoyment (bZod37igzQk @ 9:36-19:44).
- E tier: foam rolling. May ease soreness, does not cost muscle, better as a warm-up than a recovery tool (bZod37igzQk @ 5:11-9:18).
- F tier: cold water immersion for people maximizing hypertrophy; it can blunt growth. Use only between closely spaced competitions (bZod37igzQk @ 2:12-4:49).

## Where videos disagree
- Reactive vs scheduled deloads: Nippard prefers proactive; RP's progress-based triggers lean reactive but endorse both (LT_aBQatj5s @ 5:45; 2e6N9nITAYg @ 24:34).
- Week off: Nippard recommends modified training; RP says fine for hypertrophy but less so for strength; one study shows equal hypertrophy with a week off (LT_aBQatj5s @ 2:25; Dt6ok24VBpg @ 17:47; bZod37igzQk @ 24:49).
- Beginner cutoff for deloads: first year (Nippard) vs six months to three years (RP).
- Deload load: Nippard keeps loads in a taper; RP keeps load in method 1 but halves it in method 2.

## Rules the engine could use
Rules are for the progression engine. Thresholds marked [inference] are my translation of qualitative statements.

1. **Stall detection.** Input: for each lift, best set (load, reps, RPE) per session. Condition: current session fails to beat the previous exposure (more load or more reps at the same load) in 2 consecutive sessions where RPE >= 9 was logged. Action: set `deloadDue = true` for the next week (RP, Dt6ok24VBpg @ 6:50; 3lrxQ3nHOmA @ 9:13).
2. **Do not deload on one bad day.** Input: single session under-performance or readiness drop. Action: lower that day's load or sets, but do not start a deload week unless rule 1 or rule 9 confirms (RP, Dt6ok24VBpg @ 6:41) [inference for the "lower that day" part].
3. **Load increment gate.** Input: all prescribed sets completed, session RPE, previous session RPE. Condition: add load only if the new load is expected to leave RPE at the previous value or one higher (never more than +1), and logged RPE for the last session was <= 9. Action: +2.5 to 5 lb default, +10 to 15 lb for deadlift-type lifts; if a set was RPE 10, hold load (RP, 9d7NI_entvg @ 2:42-3:40, 4:12).
4. **Reps fall after a load bump.** Condition: load increased and reps drop below target on 2 consecutive sessions. Action: deload (RP, 9d7NI_entvg @ 5:41; Dt6ok24VBpg @ 6:50).
5. **Technique gate for load.** Input: user technique flag per lift (clean / sloppy). Condition: flag is sloppy. Action: repeat the same load next session; resume increments after a clean one (RP, -8Sgczn4VsM @ 3:33).
6. **Training-age gate.** Input: months training and whether the lift has plateaued. Condition: never plateaued (or < 12 months) = beginner. Action: no scheduled deload, reactive triggers only, 5-10 reps, no RPE 10 prescriptions, whole-body 3-4x/week (Nippard, LT_aBQatj5s @ 6:00; RP, -8Sgczn4VsM @ 0:13, 4:05).
7. **Scheduled deload.** Condition: intermediate or advanced and accumulation weeks since last deload reaches the limit. Action: insert a deload week. Limit: 6 weeks intermediate, 4-5 weeks advanced, range 4-8 [inference for the default] (Nippard, LT_aBQatj5s @ 4:56; RP, Dt6ok24VBpg @ 0:50; 9d7NI_entvg @ 0:54).
8. **Deload prescription (default, hypertrophy).** Action: sets x0.5 rounded up; reps x0.5 and load unchanged for the first half, load x0.5 for the second half; RPE cap 6 (about 4 reps in reserve) [inference for cap]; no PR attempts (RP, Dt6ok24VBpg @ 9:13-10:34, 20:34; Nippard, LT_aBQatj5s @ 3:36).
9. **Deload prescription (strength lifts).** Action: first half load -10%, sets x0.5; second half load x0.5, sets x0.5; keep technique practice, never zero training. Advanced or joint pain: load x0.5 all week (RP, Dt6ok24VBpg @ 15:05-17:39).
10. **Taper.** Condition: user schedules a max test on date D. Action: week before D, sets x0.5, drop accessories, keep loads (Nippard, LT_aBQatj5s @ 2:48-3:34).
11. **Lagging-indicator count.** Inputs: daily check-ins for desire to train, mood, appetite, sleep disturbance, illness, joint pain; all 1-5 or yes/no. Condition: 2 or more lagging indicators in a week [inference threshold] plus any concurrent indicator (rule 1, slow bar, or unexplained heavy feel). Action: start a deload within 1-2 days instead of waiting (RP, 3lrxQ3nHOmA @ 17:27-18:16).
12. **Illness.** Condition: user reports illness during accumulation. Action: stop progression, deload, resume progression after recovery (RP, 3lrxQ3nHOmA @ 15:53-16:10).
13. **Leading-indicator watch.** Inputs: missed meals or under-eating for several days, several days of poor sleep or high stress, previous week volume well above normal. Condition: any present. Action: show "fatigue risk" and do not add load or sets this week [inference for the action] (RP, 3lrxQ3nHOmA @ 3:12-4:58).
14. **Technique decay triggers regression, not progression.** Condition: technique flag sloppy on 2 consecutive sessions on a lift where load did not change. Action: do not advance complexity; treat as a fatigue indicator (RP, 3lrxQ3nHOmA @ 5:06) [inference: threshold].
15. **Jump-height watch.** Input: weekly jump test for users with a jump goal. Condition: height below the best of the last 3 tests. Action: flag fatigue; if also rule 1, deload (RP, 3lrxQ3nHOmA @ 5:54).
16. **Joint pain on an exercise.** Condition: joint pain during or next day on a lift that worsens set to set. Action: lower load, or swap to a variation with better SFR; record the exercise as high-fatigue (RP, i69D6PW785s @ 11:43-12:53).
17. **Post-deload cycle tuning.** Input: final accumulation week RPE and readiness. Condition: user finished fresh (RPE <= 8 and readiness >= 4) [inference thresholds]. Action: add one set per exercise or extend the cycle by one week (RP, 9d7NI_entvg @ 4:44-5:25).
18. **Deload failure check.** Condition: readiness at end of deload week is not higher than at start, or user logged RPE >= 8 in a deload. Action: tell the user it was too hard and make the next deload easier (RP, Dt6ok24VBpg @ 20:01-20:34).
19. **Set progression by tier.** Advanced lifters: hold sets, progress load only; intermediates: progress sets from minimum to maximum recoverable volume across a cycle, then hold sets once load alone fills the range (RP, -8Sgczn4VsM @ 7:56-8:52, 12:29).
20. **Recovery suggestions priority.** Order: sleep, food (deficit under about 500 kcal and protein near 1.5 g/kg), stress, deload, then sauna. Do not suggest cold immersion after hypertrophy sessions or foam-rolling homework (bZod37igzQk @ 2:12, 5:11, 28:51-31:41).

## Where the current engine disagrees with these videos
Engine today: readiness = round((energy + (6 - soreness) + sleep)/3), minus 1 if any pain; load +5% (minimum +2.5 lb) after hitting every set; two missed sessions or readiness <= 2 triggers deload (10% load, one set fewer); complexity progresses after 3 clean sessions.

- **Missed sessions are not fatigue.** The engine deloads after two missed sessions. The videos trigger on two consecutive sessions of no improvement while trying hard, not on absences (RP, Dt6ok24VBpg @ 6:50; 3lrxQ3nHOmA @ 9:35). Absences reduce fatigue; a deload after them adds more detraining.
- **No stall trigger.** Nothing in the engine detects "flat or falling for two sessions". It only sees full completion or miss (RP, Dt6ok24VBpg @ 6:50; 9d7NI_entvg @ 5:41).
- **Readiness <= 2 on one day should not become a deload week.** The videos require two-session confirmation and treat a bad day as possible noise; use it to scale that day instead (RP, Dt6ok24VBpg @ 6:41) [inference].
- **Deload is too mild.** "10% load, one set fewer" keeps the week at hard-training level. RP says hit the brakes hard (sets x0.5 plus load cuts, usually 50%), Nippard cuts 30-50% of volume and 2 RPE; one fewer set from 3 is a 33% cut with no RPE change (RP, Dt6ok24VBpg @ 4:39, 15:05; Nippard, LT_aBQatj5s @ 3:43).
- **Deload is flat.** The videos use two halves, with load or volume falling further in the second half (RP, Dt6ok24VBpg @ 9:13, 15:28).
- **No scheduled deload.** The engine is purely reactive; both channels schedule one every 4-8 weeks for non-beginners, and RP says keep it even when you feel good (Nippard, LT_aBQatj5s @ 4:56; RP, 2e6N9nITAYg @ 27:30).
- **No training-age gate.** Beginners (about the first year, or until a plateau) need no formal deloads and different rep, RPE, volume and frequency prescriptions; advanced lifters progress load only (Nippard, LT_aBQatj5s @ 6:00; RP, -8Sgczn4VsM @ 0:13, 4:05, 12:29).
- **+5% after hitting every set ignores effort.** The videos add load only while RPE stays the same or rises by one, performance must beat last time without RPE 10, and 5% is large on a 300 lb squat (15 lb, deadlift territory) while "try less first" (RP, 9d7NI_entvg @ 2:42-4:36).
- **No technique gate.** Load goes up regardless of form; RP withholds load when technique degraded (RP, -8Sgczn4VsM @ 3:33).
- **Readiness has too few inputs.** It omits nutrition, stress over several days, previous-week load, desire to train, mood, appetite and illness; soreness, sleep and energy are mostly lagging or concurrent proxies. Pain subtracts only 1, while joint or wear-and-tear pain is a serious lagging signal (RP, 3lrxQ3nHOmA @ 3:12, 11:40-16:10).
- **No performance-based indicators.** Bar speed, rep drop-off at fixed load and jump height are stronger signals than self-rating (RP, 3lrxQ3nHOmA @ 5:54, 7:06, 8:40).
- **Complexity progression ignores fatigue.** The engine advances after 3 clean sessions; the videos say technique is the first thing fatigue erodes, and beginners should stay with a few basics until technique is stable (RP, 3lrxQ3nHOmA @ 5:06; -8Sgczn4VsM @ 3:16). It also lacks regression when technique decays.
- **No exercise-level SFR or joint-pain handling.** The engine has no way to flag a high-fatigue exercise or swap it (RP, i69D6PW785s @ 17:37, 22:01).
- **No strength-vs-hypertrophy deload split, taper or post-deload tuning** (RP, Dt6ok24VBpg @ 15:05; Nippard, LT_aBQatj5s @ 2:48; RP, 9d7NI_entvg @ 4:44).
