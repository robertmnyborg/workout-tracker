# Technique principles (RP + Jeff Nippard)

Sources: RP "15 Squat Mistakes" (Fs6GwjGHKRo), RP "10 Lunge Mistakes" (0MZd3iKzzPM), RP "Proper Lifting Technique" (KSJYAEUvRv0), Nippard conventional deadlift (VL5Ab0T07e4). The RP squat and lunge videos teach for hypertrophy (stimulus-to-fatigue), not powerlifting. New records: barbell-back-squat, walking-dumbbell-lunge (library/yt-technique.json).

## What "proper technique" means
- Use the most muscle possible, in its strongest positions, to move the most load (RP, KSJYAEUvRv0 @ 0:20).
- Technique is chosen for leverage: the deadlift bar stays close to the body; coaches adjust knees, hands and stance to the lifter's build (RP, KSJYAEUvRv0 @ 1:50).
- Reject technique that is needlessly fatiguing or unsafe, such as dive-bombing squats or a fully rounded "candy cane" deadlift back (RP, KSJYAEUvRv0 @ 2:32).
- Standardize so progress is trackable: same hand, hip, foot and head positions every session; change technique slowly and for a long time, not once (RP, KSJYAEUvRv0 @ 4:10).
- Brace: breathe in, bear down around the whole abdomen; breathing out while descending is a bad idea (RP, KSJYAEUvRv0 @ 6:35).
- Stability: set the feet, lock the shoulders back before squatting, settle the grip, use chalk and flat, firm shoes; taking an extra 30 seconds to set up costs nothing (RP, KSJYAEUvRv0 @ 7:23).
- Meet the standard of the target lift (full bench ROM, pause). Assistance work must clearly load the intended muscle or weak range, for example a deficit big enough to limit you (RP, KSJYAEUvRv0 @ 9:39).

## Squat (hypertrophy framing)
- Depth: do not cut range arbitrarily; stretched quads, glutes and adductors grow more (RP, Fs6GwjGHKRo @ 0:30). Do not go lower than you can keep a neutral spine and flat feet; just below parallel is a good squat, and ass-to-grass only comes with mobility over time (RP, Fs6GwjGHKRo @ 4:47).
- Pick one depth landmark and hit it every rep so strength gains are comparable (RP, Fs6GwjGHKRo @ 10:52).
- Lower back: keep the spine relatively straight, chest not caving; resist gravity folding you at the bottom (RP, Fs6GwjGHKRo @ 1:32).
- Heels rising: think back a little and then down, stay on the whole foot, or pause at the bottom and settle onto the heels (RP, Fs6GwjGHKRo @ 2:47).
- Bracing: breathe in at the top, tighten, breathe out in the last half or at the top. Over-bracing on 5-15 rep sets wastes energy that should go to the quads (RP, Fs6GwjGHKRo @ 6:13).
- Eccentric: controlled lowering, no dive bombing; 4-5 second tempo optional (RP, Fs6GwjGHKRo @ 9:37).
- Bar position: high bar for quad hypertrophy (lower stimulus-to-fatigue with low bar). Use a pad or safety squat bar if the bar hurts your neck or shoulders, and wean off later (RP, Fs6GwjGHKRo @ 12:18).
- Stance and toe angle: any width is fine if you get full range, heels stay down, back stays neutral, you feel it in the quads, and joints feel fine (RP, Fs6GwjGHKRo @ 16:40).
- Forward lean: knees past toes is fine if hips and knees break together; long femur and short torso lifters can widen the stance and flare the toes (RP, Fs6GwjGHKRo @ 18:45).
- Feeling quads: pause at the bottom, let the knees slide forward and stay upright, slow the eccentric (RP, Fs6GwjGHKRo @ 21:13).
- Load: 5-15 reps for quad growth; under 5 is high systemic fatigue, over 15 becomes cardio and spinal stabilization, so leave 15-30 reps to hack squat, leg press and leg extension (RP, Fs6GwjGHKRo @ 23:02).
- Within RP: low bar lets most people lift more weight and is the better choice for maximal strength (KSJYAEUvRv0 @ 1:26), while high bar is preferred for quad hypertrophy (Fs6GwjGHKRo @ 12:18).

## Lunge (walking dumbbell, hypertrophy framing)
- Target: glutes, adductors, quads (RP, 0MZd3iKzzPM @ 0:39).
- Range: back knee gently touches the floor every rep; same range each rep (RP, 0MZd3iKzzPM @ 1:27 and 3:08).
- Step length about a yard (a metre) to start; too short is squat-like with knee shear, too long destabilizes (RP, 0MZd3iKzzPM @ 5:04).
- Controlled descent of about two seconds (RP, 0MZd3iKzzPM @ 7:48).
- Stay upright, do not tilt forward and hand work to the back and hamstrings (RP, 0MZd3iKzzPM @ 9:06).
- Stopping the trailing leg beside the lead leg is allowed and often better than swinging it through (RP, 0MZd3iKzzPM @ 10:44).
- Grip or traps limiting you: use chalk, straps, or Versa Grips, or switch to barbell lunges (RP, 0MZd3iKzzPM @ 12:28).
- Rep range about 15-30 steps; over 30-40 per set means add load, under 10-15 is unstable and caps force (RP, 0MZd3iKzzPM @ 14:29).
- Check the target muscle limits the set: burn, pump and disruption in the glutes; if only quads are cooked, vary step length, load or direction, or drop lunges (RP, 0MZd3iKzzPM @ 16:59).

## Conventional deadlift
- Frequency: once or twice a week at most; 5-10 reps for hypertrophy, 1-5 for strength (Nippard, VL5Ab0T07e4 @ 2:11).
- Compared with sumo: more spinal erector and hip demand, torso leans further forward (Nippard, VL5Ab0T07e4 @ 0:33).

## Cues for existing exercises

### barbell-deadlift
- Shins about half an inch behind the bar, bar over the shoelaces, stance slightly narrower than shoulder width, toes straight ahead (Nippard, VL5Ab0T07e4 @ 2:51).
- Set hips back to take the grip, then let the knees come forward until the shins lightly touch the bar without knocking it forward (Nippard, VL5Ab0T07e4 @ 3:16).
- Arms straight, hands just outside the shins at shoulder width; alternate grip lets you pull more but alternate the overhand hand, double overhand limits strength, or use hook or straps for some sets, with at least one heavy set per session without straps (Nippard, VL5Ab0T07e4 @ 3:32).
- Drive the pinky into the bar, pack the lats by thinking of pulling the bar into you (Nippard, VL5Ab0T07e4 @ 4:20).
- Pull the lower back to neutral, stretch the hamstrings, keep the hips high and tilted, big breath into the gut (Nippard, VL5Ab0T07e4 @ 4:36).
- Scapula, mid-bar and mid-foot line up in a straight line (Nippard, VL5Ab0T07e4 @ 5:08).
- Pull the slack out of the bar until it bends before the plates leave the floor; do not grip and rip (Nippard, VL5Ab0T07e4 @ 5:15).
- Explode up with the chest leading, drive hips forward, hold the lumbar and scapulae isometrically, keep the bar dragging up the shins in a straight line (Nippard, VL5Ab0T07e4 @ 6:04).
- Lockout: hips and knees straight, chest up, shoulders slightly back; no leaning back, hyperextending or shrugging (Nippard, VL5Ab0T07e4 @ 6:36).
- Descent: hips back first with straight knees, then bend the knees as the bar clears them; do not push the bar forward to clear the knees; reset fully on the floor without bouncing (Nippard, VL5Ab0T07e4 @ 7:02).
- Error: all-the-way rounded back ("candy cane") lifts a lot but raises spinal injury risk; advanced lifters stay tight and let the hips work (RP, KSJYAEUvRv0 @ 3:06).
- Error: bar drifting away from the legs loses leverage (RP, KSJYAEUvRv0 @ 1:59).

### barbell-front-squat
- The RP squat cues for depth, neutral back, heels flat, bracing and a controlled eccentric apply; the video is shot with a back squat, so treat these as transferred [inference] (RP, Fs6GwjGHKRo @ 0:30).
- Heels rising: sit back a little while unlocking the knees, stay on the whole foot, pause at the bottom (RP, Fs6GwjGHKRo @ 2:47).
- Hit the same depth landmark every rep (RP, Fs6GwjGHKRo @ 10:52).
- Breathe in at the top, brace, breathe out in the last half or at the top (RP, Fs6GwjGHKRo @ 7:30).
- Knees may travel past toes when hips and knees break together (RP, Fs6GwjGHKRo @ 19:43).

### ffe-bulgarian-split-squat-hold
- Cues transferred from the RP lunge video; its subject was walking lunges [inference] (RP, 0MZd3iKzzPM @ 0:39).
- Reach full depth with a gentle touch of the knee or bottom position, same depth every rep (RP, 0MZd3iKzzPM @ 1:27 and 3:08).
- Control the descent, about two seconds (RP, 0MZd3iKzzPM @ 7:48).
- Torso upright, no forward tilt to recruit the back and hamstrings (RP, 0MZd3iKzzPM @ 9:06).
- Step length: too short loads the knee with shear, too long destabilizes (RP, 0MZd3iKzzPM @ 5:04).
- Check you feel the glutes, adductors and quads and not only the quads (RP, 0MZd3iKzzPM @ 16:59).

### barbell-rdl
- Hips back first with straight knees, bar tracking the legs: the deadlift lowering phase is described as a "stiff leg deadlift" hinge (Nippard, VL5Ab0T07e4 @ 7:19).

## Rules the engine could use
- Log one depth standard per squat or lunge and flag sets where the user marks reps as inconsistent in depth (RP, Fs6GwjGHKRo @ 10:52; 0MZd3iKzzPM @ 3:08).
- Squat hypertrophy sets: prescribe 5-15 reps; do not prescribe squats above 15 reps, route 15-30 reps to leg press, hack squat or leg extension (RP, Fs6GwjGHKRo @ 23:02).
- Walking lunges: prescribe 15-30 steps; raise load if a set exceeds 30-40 steps (RP, 0MZd3iKzzPM @ 14:29).
- Deadlift: no more than 2 sessions per week; 5-10 reps for hypertrophy, 1-5 for strength (Nippard, VL5Ab0T07e4 @ 2:11).
- If heels rise or the lower back rounds at the bottom, cap depth at the last clean rep (RP, Fs6GwjGHKRo @ 5:42).
- If grip or traps end a dumbbell lunge set before the glutes do, suggest straps or a barbell lunge (RP, 0MZd3iKzzPM @ 13:59).
- Keep technique fixed for a full block when tracking progress; change it only slowly (RP, KSJYAEUvRv0 @ 6:19).
