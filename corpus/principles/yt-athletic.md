# Athletic training principles (RP + Jeff Nippard)

Sources: RP "Why Pro Athletes Suck at Weight Training" (nYIczQYRx4I), RP "Erling Haaland" critique (4-o4WwmfaeE), RP "ALL Athletes Must Run" (YB8BaCtZjIs), Jeff Nippard "Pro NFL Football Workout" with John Meadows (cPsy38pkZzE), RP Lecture 29 "Training for Sports" (2nbDIrDnBjY), RP Hoffmann "Testing and Monitoring" (c7b_gh6Th18), RP Hoffmann "Peaking and Tapering" (sZWfY-wEaY8). Most are lecture or reaction videos, so there are few exercises.

## Why lift at all, and what carries over
- Sport training is what gets you good at the sport; resistance training (mostly sets of 5-10, a few sets, a few times a week) is what makes you jump higher, sprint faster and get hurt less (RP, 4-o4WwmfaeE @ 2:32).
- For vertical jumping and sprinting, vertical force is the limiter, so the priority is squatting: deep squats for sets of about 5, or leg press / hack squat; "increase that number and you're going to be able to jump higher" (RP, 4-o4WwmfaeE @ 4:14).
- The posterior chain (hamstrings, glutes, back) drives pulling along the ground and changing direction, and cuts hamstring injuries (RP, 4-o4WwmfaeE @ 4:38). The anterior chain (abs, hip flexors) supports running technique and kicking (RP, 4-o4WwmfaeE @ 3:17). [inference] For basketball: squat pattern first, posterior chain second.

## Exercise selection and how to lift for sport
- Sport training is not forgiving the way physique training is: volume, intensity, frequency and exercise choice have to be narrowed so the lifting transfers to the sport's key performance indicators (RP, 2nbDIrDnBjY @ 7:58).
- Main lifts: heavy compound barbell movements (pressing, squatting, pulling, weightlifting derivatives) in the 5-10 rep range. Reps above about 15 carry over very little, and 10-20 rep sets produce only 4-5 truly powerful reps before junk volume (RP, 2nbDIrDnBjY @ 14:06, 31:41).
- Move with maximal intent on every rep even when the bar is slow, to push the force-velocity profile to the right (RP, 2nbDIrDnBjY @ 40:23).
- High-rep, low-load training shifts motor units toward slow-twitch, fatigue-resistant behavior, which is the opposite of what a jumper wants; get sport endurance from conditioning and practice (RP, 2nbDIrDnBjY @ 32:18).
- Squat depth for explosive work can be partial: "no real application to a full squat" for a linebacker, so contrast work uses a sport-relevant range (Jeff Nippard, cPsy38pkZzE @ 9:30). Disagreement: RP recommends deep squats for strength (4-o4WwmfaeE @ 4:22); reconcile as deep squats in the strength block, partial range in the explosive circuit [inference].

## Explosive and contrast work
- Contrast / French contrast circuit: heavy-ish squat, then a jump, then a banded jump, then a banded swing. Squat load only about 50-55% of max for sets of 4; wait about 20 seconds so the potentiation is not lost; 4 rounds (Jeff Nippard, cPsy38pkZzE @ 6:19, 7:09, 7:39).
- Train explosively while somewhat fatigued so it transfers to games where you are never fully rested; the fourth round loses coordination and speed (Jeff Nippard, cPsy38pkZzE @ 7:47, 16:21).
- Jump technique: arms low then swing back as the counterbalance, drive force into the ground, then explode (Jeff Nippard, cPsy38pkZzE @ 8:55). Land softly rather than flat-footed (cPsy38pkZzE @ 5:52).

## Combining lifting with sport
- Lifting makes you tired and sore for practice in the short term, which is why athletes drop it; the payoff comes months later, and dropping lifting near important games is the correct use of that fatigue (RP, nYIczQYRx4I @ 13:01, 15:24).
- Skill and speed/power work are far more fatigue-sensitive than hypertrophy work; when preparedness is low, extra training is junk volume (RP, 2nbDIrDnBjY @ 24:39, 25:20).
- Adding lifting means cutting something else: lower MRV for both local and systemic, drop each muscle's frequency by half to one session per week, aim for MAV instead of MRV (RP, 2nbDIrDnBjY @ 34:57, 38:18, 39:50).
- You cannot do your normal sport volume and add lifting on top; dial the others down (RP, 2nbDIrDnBjY @ 44:36).
- Body composition change belongs in the general preparation phase, far from competition and before specific prep (RP, 2nbDIrDnBjY @ 27:52). Lighter is better for jumping; most sports have an optimal competition weight, not "as big and lean as possible" (RP, 2nbDIrDnBjY @ 18:23).
- Consolidate stressors: keep legs-heavy lifting and high-intensity cardio on separate days with space; low-intensity cardio can share a day with lifting so other days stay free (RP, 2nbDIrDnBjY @ 42:12).
- Volume spikes are among the most reliable injury predictors; avoid a layoff then a sudden ramp, keep smooth curves and never fully stop the sport (RP, 4-o4WwmfaeE @ 6:39, 7:09).
- Conditioning: sport-specific drills (ball handling, small-sided games, shuttles) beat jogging because they train skill, change of direction and conditioning together. Jogging is fine when equipment is limited or as monotony relief (RP, YB8BaCtZjIs @ 7:25, 2:53, 4:35).

## Testing and monitoring
- Testing is periodic (2-3 times a year at most), precise, comparative against norms. Monitoring is continuous, simple, field-based (RP, c7b_gh6Th18 @ 13:35).
- Jumps and sprints are among the best general athleticism indices; use countermovement and squat jumps (chalk wall touch is better than nothing) and 5/10/20 m sprints with a stopwatch (RP, c7b_gh6Th18 @ 12:07, 29:57, 36:07).
- For monitoring strength, use training logs: if your 5-rep load goes up, you got stronger without a 1RM test (RP, c7b_gh6Th18 @ 35:04).
- Baseline any variable for 2-4 weeks, take the mean, set bounds at about 2 standard deviations; one point outside is noise, several in a row means act (RP, c7b_gh6Th18 @ 22:27, 24:46).
- Do not test too often; testing steals training and recovery time (RP, c7b_gh6Th18 @ 44:04). Be skeptical of unvalidated sport-specific tests (RP, c7b_gh6Th18 @ 43:17).

## Peaking and tapering
- A taper cuts fatigue while holding fitness; do not stop training in the taper because adaptive decay starts (RP, sZWfY-wEaY8 @ 7:13).
- Volume is the non-negotiable variable: reduce total volume from every source (lifting, practice, conditioning, scrimmage) to 41-60% of pre-taper (RP, sZWfY-wEaY8 @ 30:19, 29:39).
- Keep intensity the same or higher so the fitness is held; only drop intensity in the last one or two sessions (RP, sZWfY-wEaY8 @ 28:04, 37:28).
- Two weeks is a safe default for intermediates; beginners (<2 years) can get by with a one-week deload; elite strength athletes may need three (RP, sZWfY-wEaY8 @ 34:05).
- Example for a Saturday event: week 1 cut volume (3x3 to 3x2 or 2x3, same relative intensity), week 2 first half 2x2, then final session 2x1 or 1x2 with relative intensity down 15-20% (RP, sZWfY-wEaY8 @ 39:27, 40:55).
- Taper only for competitions that matter; use a one-week deload otherwise; at most two or three full tapers a year (RP, sZWfY-wEaY8 @ 11:36, 43:28).

## Cues for existing exercises

### vertical-jump-practice
- Arms low, counterbalance swings back, drive force into the ground, then explode (Jeff Nippard, cPsy38pkZzE @ 8:55).
- Land gently, absorbing force rather than landing flat-footed (Jeff Nippard, cPsy38pkZzE @ 5:52).
- Max-effort jumps are best early in a round; quality drops with fatigue (Jeff Nippard, cPsy38pkZzE @ 16:06).

### kb-swing
- Use a lighter load and generate as much force as possible on each rep (Jeff Nippard, cPsy38pkZzE @ 8:12).
- Band resistance rewards explosive follow-through (Jeff Nippard, cPsy38pkZzE @ 11:22).
- Arms back and drop down as fast as you can (Jeff Nippard, cPsy38pkZzE @ 17:02).

## Rules the engine could use
1. Taper volume: in the 14 days before a priority game, cut total weekly training volume (lifting + practice + conditioning) to 41-60% of the pre-taper week (RP, sZWfY-wEaY8 @ 30:19).
2. Taper intensity: keep lifting intensity within 100-105% of pre-taper loads until the final one or two sessions, then drop relative intensity 15-20% and set count to 1-2 (RP, sZWfY-wEaY8 @ 37:28, 40:55).
3. No full rest: forbid zero-training taper days earlier than the last two sessions (RP, sZWfY-wEaY8 @ 7:13).
4. Taper scarcity: allow a full taper only for flagged priority games, at most 2-3 per year; use a 1-week deload otherwise (RP, sZWfY-wEaY8 @ 43:28, 11:36).
5. Lifting load cap with sport: when sport practice is added, lower each muscle's weekly lifting frequency by 0.5-1 session and target MAV not MRV (RP, 2nbDIrDnBjY @ 39:50, 35:11).
6. Rep range: main lifts at 5-10 reps; never above 15; log intent to move fast (RP, 2nbDIrDnBjY @ 31:41).
7. Hard-day spacing: do not schedule heavy leg lifting and high-intensity intervals on the same day; allow low-intensity cardio with lifting (RP, 2nbDIrDnBjY @ 42:42, 43:08).
8. Volume ramp guard: flag weeks where total volume jumps sharply from the previous week or after a layoff (RP, 4-o4WwmfaeE @ 6:39).
9. Monitoring band: baseline jump height and body weight for 2-4 weeks; mean +/- 2 SD; if 3 or more consecutive points fall outside, recommend a lighter week (RP, c7b_gh6Th18 @ 24:46). The "3" is an inference from "two, three, four points".
10. Test cadence: formal jump/sprint test sessions no more than 2-3 per year; between them use the cheap field jump and logged lift numbers (RP, c7b_gh6Th18 @ 44:04, 35:04).
11. Contrast session cap: explosive contrast circuits at most twice a week, never the day before a game, separated from heavy lifting (Jeff Nippard, cPsy38pkZzE @ 14:43; the day-before rule is an inference).
12. Conditioning default: prefer sport-specific drills over jogging unless equipment is limited (RP, YB8BaCtZjIs @ 7:25).
