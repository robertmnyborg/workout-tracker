## 1. Spacing high-intensity jump days (the "72 hour myth")
- Tendon recovery has no fixed number. It depends on the dose: a big dose can take 72 hours or longer, less damage takes 48, and even less takes 24 (PJF, NF658w-s8io @ 3:24-3:58).
- Tendon and cartilage adapt more slowly than muscle, but their recovery also depends on dose (PJF, NF658w-s8io @ 6:24).
- In his mid-30s, Paul needs a long warm-up before a 100% jump. He therefore prefers a higher dose on Monday and Thursday over small daily doses (PJF, NF658w-s8io @ 7:07-7:56).
- **Rollover method.** Suppose your muscles are still sore on the day of an intense or moderate lower-body lift, jump or sprint session. Assume the tendons and cartilage have not healed either, and push the session one day. Repeat each morning after the warm-up (PJF, NF658w-s8io @ 8:53-9:49).
- **Target:** recover within 48 hours of a session. If you don't, cut sets by 50% and ramp back up until the full session still leaves you recovered for the next one (PJF, NF658w-s8io @ 9:49-10:19).
- Fill in-between days with low-intensity, low-impact zone 2 cardio, core, mobility, and shooting (PJF, NF658w-s8io @ 10:40-10:56). Most PJF programs put lower body on Mon/Wed/Fri and low-intensity cardio, core and mobility on Tue/Thu/Sat (PJF, NF658w-s8io @ 13:30).
- Back-to-backs come only after a build-up, and both days stay low volume. The strength day must cause no soreness, and sprint/plyo volume drops "significantly". PJF's highest frequency anywhere: strength Mon, plyo Tue, Wed off, strength Thu, plyo Fri, weekend off (PJF, NF658w-s8io @ 11:46-13:21).
- TMS's 12-week program uses the same anchors. Monday and Thursday are the hardest lower-body power days, kept "the farthest apart". Tuesday is conditioning (EMOM, 20-40 s work, 30-40 min total), Wednesday is optional upper body, Friday is mobility, and Saturday is optional full body. The program trims to 2-3 days (TMS, 1CFAZfxEkoQ @ 3:02-5:13).
- Agreement: both coaches give an adult Mon/Thu max-intensity days with 2-3 days between them. [inference] Treat ≥48 h between high-intensity jump days as the default, with 72 h preferred.

## 2. Volume: foot contacts by level and intensity
- A contact is one foot strike: 10 countermovement jumps = 10 contacts, and each bound or single-leg hop counts (TMS, zLXua2DzQ1o @ 0:44).
- Old CSCS 4th-edition limits per session: beginner 80-100, intermediate 100-120, advanced 120-140, with no intensity adjustment (TMS, zLXua2DzQ1o @ 0:32).
- CSCS 5th edition splits each level into low, moderate and high intensity. Figures the transcript states (all **per session**):

| Level (plyo training age) | Low | Moderate | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beginner (<2 months) | 80-100 | not read out | none (TMS @ 1:39) |
| Intermediate (2-6 months) | not read out | not read out | 0-20 (TMS @ 9:07) |
| Advanced (~1 year) | 150 | 80 | 30 (TMS @ 2:41-3:20) |

  Training-age bands come from TMS @ 4:44-5:00. TMS reads the advanced numbers as examples of single-category sessions, not as a full chart; the other cells appear only on screen.
- Pick **one** range per session, not all three. A blended session scales each range down: "80 low and 50-60 moderate" for an advanced athlete (TMS, zLXua2DzQ1o @ 2:57-3:54).
- Intensity scales inversely with contacts across the year. The off-season uses 100-120 mostly low-intensity contacts (pogos, split jumps, skips, stadium jumps, bounds at 70-90%). The preseason uses ~50 mostly moderate contacts (band-assisted jumps, hurdle hops, reactive lateral hops, approach jumps). In-season uses 25-30 moderate-to-high contacts (TMS, zLXua2DzQ1o @ 7:27-8:05, 9:48-10:03, 12:22-12:40).
- 3x10 depth jumps (30 high contacts) is a programming error for an intermediate athlete, and worse when stacked on a lot of low-intensity volume (TMS, zLXua2DzQ1o @ 8:59-9:23).
- The CSCS video gives no weekly totals. PJF's weekly figure for jump priority at age 35: "50 plus maximal jumps per week", or "at least 30-50" (PJF, mNbpZ9-7XM0 @ 4:03-4:36).
- Max-effort, longer-contact plyos (250-500 ms: max bounds, approach jumps, loaded jumps) make up 10-30% of TMS's plyo programs (TMS, xeR4RprByc4 @ 7:23-7:39).
- To keep fast plyos short-contact, cue 60%, then 70%, then 80% effort (TMS, xeR4RprByc4 @ 10:30).

## 3. The tendon jerk budget (pickup basketball counts)
- Jerk is the rate of change of acceleration. A slow controlled squat has little jerk. Fast drop-and-reverse moves, catching cleans, change of direction, jumping and sprinting carry a lot. "You can only handle so much fast pulling on that tendon before it flares up" (PJF, mNbpZ9-7XM0 @ 0:31-1:10).
- Slow heavy lifting trains tendons differently and does not draw on the budget (PJF, mNbpZ9-7XM0 @ 1:10-1:18).
- The budget has three buckets: on-court basketball, max jumps plus sprints, and fast lifting (Olympic variations, trap bar jumps, speed squats).
  - **Durable athlete:** high / medium / low. With basketball high, keep jumps and sprints medium and fast lifting low or zero (PJF, mNbpZ9-7XM0 @ 2:50-3:30).
  - **Jump priority:** jumps high (30-50+/week), basketball medium, fast lifting low (PJF, mNbpZ9-7XM0 @ 4:03-4:54).
  - **Less durable or injury-prone athlete:** high in one bucket, low in the other two. In season that means high basketball, low jump/sprint maintenance, and zero fast lifting (PJF, mNbpZ9-7XM0 @ 5:02-5:18, 9:01).
- Players high in all three buckets "spend their entire year rehabbing" (PJF, mNbpZ9-7XM0 @ 5:27). A 33/33/33 split maintains each quality and improves none (PJF, mNbpZ9-7XM0 @ 3:30-3:55).
- Keep a sprint day once a week to hold speed (PJF, mNbpZ9-7XM0 @ 3:05).
- **When court volume can't drop:** do the max jumps in game warm-ups or when bouncy after a pickup game, and do nothing fast in the weight room (PJF, mNbpZ9-7XM0 @ 6:46-7:09).
- When on-court volume is high, low-landing-force concentric drills (trap bar jumps onto boxes, box jumps) still build explosiveness (PJF, AR1JI0RFsbQ @ 3:36-3:52).
- What to cut after he played: the videos give no contacts-per-minute conversion for basketball. [inference] Count a hard session with jumping as that day's high-jerk dose. Drop fast lifting first, then the next day's max jumps, and leave slow heavy strength alone.

## 4. Progression levels, and cranky-knee days
**Five levels** (TMS, _YudFDcW_Iw):
1. Snap downs and drop freezes teach force absorption (@ 0:15-1:31).
2. Box jumps reduce landing forces, so volume can build without patellar or Achilles trouble (@ 1:31).
3. Vertical jumps absorb a real landing and train full ankle, knee and hip extension (@ 2:05-2:44).
4. Depth jumps come after mastering vertical jumps and drop freezes. Start at 12-18 in and go to 24 in only when technique holds. Begin box-to-box so the landing impact disappears (@ 2:44-3:32). The description adds a gate for each height: the athlete must not "spend too long on the ground".
5. Depth-jump variations start with a single-leg landing hold (no hip drop, no knee to midline), held for "a few weeks". Then come lateral, vertical and tuck variations, sequenced 4-5 in a row (@ 3:32-5:16).

- TMS adds depth jumps in phase 2 or 3, only after a strength foundation (TMS, 1CFAZfxEkoQ @ 7:04-7:19).
- A poor stretch-shortening athlete (drop-jump heights flat or falling) "would get hurt" on depth jumps, hurdle jumps and bounding. Keep that athlete longer on extensive plyos: 50% bounds, submax repeat jumps, longer jump rope. Max hurdle jumps are not repeated; a double hop goes between reps (PJF, k4QD_g3nkaU @ 3:59-4:47).
- Reactive strength index (RSI) = jump height (m) / ground contact time (s). Below 1.5 is very low, 1.5-2.0 is moderate, and 3.0+ is very good (TMS, 11uS8bwkfcc @ 12:15-12:56).

**Low-readiness / cranky-knee substitutes**
- The weighted trap bar jump onto 12-inch boxes produced >2500 N on takeoff and only ~3000 N on landing. A 24-inch drop landing produced 4500 N hard and 3500 N soft. A one-step jump onto a 12-inch box landed at 2100 N, about 50% of the drop (PJF, AR1JI0RFsbQ @ 0:36-3:12).
- Limit: maximal jumping with zero impact "could still flare up patellar tendinopathy", because spring forces set it off (PJF, AR1JI0RFsbQ @ 4:00-4:22).
- Isometrics: 2-4 sets of 45 s Spanish squat before practice, a game, lifting or plyos. The pain relief lasted at least 45 minutes in Rio's study (PJF, 6oBTSuxggLc @ 5:00-6:40). Then do heavy, slow, non-explosive reps, paced by a metronome at 2 s down and 2 s up (PJF, 6oBTSuxggLc @ 7:42-7:55, 9:31-9:55).
- TMS tendon spectrum (TMS, h8zKS5qZZdY):
  - **General physical prep (GPP):** 2-3 sets of 1-3 min holds, 3-6x/week, for a sensitive or untrained tendon (@ 1:36, 4:23-4:47).
  - **Rebuild:** 20-40 s holds at an honest 7/10, about 70% of max force (@ 5:38-7:44). Thirty seconds of 2 s up / 2 s down heavy slow resistance works as well (@ 9:00).
  - **Timeline:** at least 2-3x/week for 3 months (@ 9:50-10:13).
  - **Maintenance:** 3 x 30 s, 2-3x/week (@ 10:20-10:40).
  - **Session order:** max-force isos go first, yielding holds go last (@ 13:37).
  - **Off-limits on an injured tendon:** ballistic and switch isos (@ 16:12).
- Disagreement on load: TMS says a bodyweight split squat hold won't make a patellar tendon stronger and pushes heavy weight (TMS, h8zKS5qZZdY @ 7:18). PJF uses a bodyweight Spanish squat or a goblet hold for healthy athletes, aiming at prevention and pain relief (PJF, 6oBTSuxggLc @ 9:14-9:23). [inference] Use PJF's hold as the pre-session primer and TMS's heavy holds as the tendon-building dose.
- Tendon pain right now → see a physical therapist (PJF, 6oBTSuxggLc @ 8:31).

## 5. Strength vs plyo balance, the tests, and phases
- **Standing vs approach.** From a 15-ft approach you should jump at least 4 inches higher than standing; less "chances are very very good" means mechanics (PJF, wrF0h4YadvM @ 2:04-2:29). The best separation PJF has seen is 12-13 in (@ 2:52). Fixing mechanics is the fastest way to add vertical (@ 1:42-2:04).
- **Squat jump vs countermovement jump vs drop jumps** (hands on hips). In PJF's database, pro dunkers and elite NBA and volleyball players show a CMJ 10-20% above their SJ. Drop jumps from 12 then 18 in should keep rising. Flat drop-jump heights mark an average SSC; falling heights mark an underdeveloped one, which needs plyometrics and "may not need a ton of concentric strength work" (PJF, k4QD_g3nkaU @ 0:28-3:59).
- **Dynamic strength index (DSI)** = CMJ peak force / isometric peak force. Below 0.6 → little lifting (maybe once a week) and lots of plyos. 0.6-0.8 → concurrent training. Above 0.8 → lift to raise the ceiling (TMS citing PJF, 11uS8bwkfcc @ 9:59-11:35).
- Max strength sets the upper limit, and stronger athletes respond faster to plyos (TMS, xeR4RprByc4 @ 1:49-2:04). Box, broad and loaded jumps mostly duplicate strength training and "won't make you an athlete" (TMS, xeR4RprByc4 @ 3:24-3:59). PJF agrees that the middle-ground power work is the least important (PJF, mNbpZ9-7XM0 @ 1:52, 7:09).
- **Phases** (TMS, 1CFAZfxEkoQ):
  1. **Phase 1 (4 weeks):** hypertrophy plus plyos. Do full-range front squats and dumbbell loaded jumps (10-15% bodyweight, 4x5, quarter squat) (@ 0:28-2:46).
  2. **Phase 2:** strength sets of 6-8 with higher plyo intensity: single-leg lateral barrier hops, and depth jumps in phase 2 or 3 (@ 5:29-7:19).
  3. **Phase 3 (~4 weeks):** the most specific phase: unloaded or band-assisted jumps with the shortest contact. Staying longer plateaus (@ 7:19-8:38).
  4. **Overall split:** 4 weeks of hypertrophy/strength, then 8 weeks of power/plyo (@ 9:01).
  5. **Testing:** test the vertical at the start of each week with tape or chalk on a wall. Results take ~4 weeks. Expect "up to" 1-2 in a month (@ 7:27-8:00, 9:31-9:39).
- Keep the warm-up the same before every test (TMS, 11uS8bwkfcc @ 1:32).
- Teach jump-mechanics changes early or mid off-season, so they don't compete with on-court learning (PJF, wrF0h4YadvM @ 36:50-37:13, 43:23).

## Agreements and disagreements with other corpus files
- `jump-science.md` (RP) calls tendon stiffness mostly fixed. TMS and PJF treat tendons as trainable through heavy isometrics and HSR (heavy slow resistance) over 3+ months (TMS, h8zKS5qZZdY @ 9:50). Disagreement.
- RP says "get as strong as possible" for the vertical. TMS agrees strength sets the ceiling. PJF's tests (SSC profile, DSI) say some athletes need plyos more than lifting (PJF, k4QD_g3nkaU @ 3:35). The two views agree once a test individualizes them.
- `yt-athletic.md` endorses a French contrast circuit (banded jumps, banded swings) up to twice a week. PJF ranks that fast or middle-ground lifting lowest and counts it against the jerk budget (PJF, mNbpZ9-7XM0 @ 7:09-7:34). Disagreement. RP's "dial the other stressors down when adding lifting" and its volume-spike warning agree with PJF's budget and its 50% cut-and-ramp.
- `yt-athletic.md` tests 2-3 times a year. TMS tests the vertical weekly. These two cover different jobs: formal testing versus field monitoring, which RP also endorses.
- Loaded jumps to a quarter squat (TMS @ 2:06) agree with Nippard's sport-range partial squats for explosive work.

## Cues for existing exercises
### depth-jump
- Start at 12-18 in. Move to 24 in only when technique holds and ground contact stays short (TMS, _YudFDcW_Iw @ 2:53-3:09).
- Short contact is a drop jump. Taking more time to go deeper turns it into a muscle-dominant depth jump (TMS, xeR4RprByc4 @ 5:00-5:34; 11uS8bwkfcc @ 7:08-7:25).
### vertical-jump-practice
- Throw the arms back hard to load the posterior chain. Fully extend ankle, knee and hip (TMS, _YudFDcW_Iw @ 2:13-2:44).
- Let the athlete self-organize torso angle; most do best near 45°, trap-bar-deadlift-like. Still train both upright and hip-hinge starts, for box-outs (PJF, wrF0h4YadvM @ 12:05-16:47).
- Don't cue a fast drop on a less-trained athlete. Beginners jumped lower when they dropped fast (PJF, wrF0h4YadvM @ 19:39-21:22).
### bunny-hops
- As a low-intensity pogo, cue 60%, then 70%, then 80% effort to keep contacts short and elastic (TMS, xeR4RprByc4 @ 10:30).
### countermovement-jump-test
- Test without arm swing (hands on hips) for consistency, and use an identical warm-up each time (TMS, 11uS8bwkfcc @ 1:32, 2:44-3:00; PJF, k4QD_g3nkaU @ 0:53).
### squat-jump-test
- Hold the bottom for 5 seconds (TMS, 11uS8bwkfcc @ 3:56), or use a self-selected depth (PJF, k4QD_g3nkaU @ 0:37). Compare against the CMJ: ~10% is typical, and 10-20% is the elite range (TMS, 11uS8bwkfcc @ 5:11; PJF, k4QD_g3nkaU @ 1:19).
### weighted-wall-sit
- As tendon GPP, build to 2-3 sets of 1-3 min holds, 3-6x/week. Then add load until a 20-40 s hold is a 7/10 (TMS, h8zKS5qZZdY @ 1:45, 4:23, 5:38).

## Rules the engine could use
Definitions. **HI-jump day**: any logged exercise with category plyometric or power, intensity ≥4 and impact high; or outside activity `{kind:"basketball", hard:true, jumping:true}`. **Fast-lift**: category power, intensity ≥4, and an id outside the plyometric list (e.g. trap-bar-jump-to-boxes, french-contrast-circuit, banded-kb-swing). **Max jump**: a plyometric/skill rep at intensity 5.

1. **Spacing.** Keep ≥48 h between HI-jump days. Default to Mon + Thu (PJF, NF658w-s8io @ 7:40; TMS, 1CFAZfxEkoQ @ 3:02). Allow a consecutive pair only after `weeksInProgram ≥ 4` [inference; PJF says "built up to"]. The pair is a low-volume strength day, then a plyo/sprint/dunk day at reduced volume. Allow at most 2 pairs/week, and leave the day after each pair off (PJF, NF658w-s8io @ 11:46-13:21).
2. **Rollover.** Input: `soreness(lowerBody) = true` on a day with a lower-body strength session (intensity ≥3), a plyo or a sprint. Action: move that session +1 day. Replace it with intensity ≤2, impact none/low work: zone 2 cardio, core, mobility, shooting. Re-check daily (PJF, NF658w-s8io @ 8:53-10:56).
3. **48-hour check.** If soreness from a session lasts more than 48 h, set the next session of that type to 50% of its sets. Then ramp toward full. [inference: +1 set per week] (PJF, NF658w-s8io @ 9:49-10:19).
4. **Between-day content.** On days between HI-jump days, allow only intensity ≤2, or intensity 3 with impact none: upper body, core, mobility, low-impact cardio (PJF, NF658w-s8io @ 13:30; TMS, 1CFAZfxEkoQ @ 3:02-4:34).
5. **Session contact cap.** Inputs: `plyoAgeMonths`, and each plyo's contacts mapped to a class. [inference] intensity ≤3 = low, 4 = moderate, 5 with impact high = high. The cap depends on training age:
   - **Beginner (<2 months):** low ≤100, high = 0.
   - **Intermediate (2-6 months):** high ≤20.
   - **Advanced (≥12 months):** low ≤150, moderate ≤80, high ≤30.
   - **Mixed sessions:** require Σ(contacts/cap) ≤ ~1.1 [inference from "80 low + 50-60 moderate"].
   (TMS, zLXua2DzQ1o @ 1:39, 2:41-3:54, 4:44-5:00, 9:07)
6. **Weekly max jumps.** With `goal = jump` and `durable = true`, target 30-50 max jumps/week, including dunk-session and approach-jump reps (PJF, mNbpZ9-7XM0 @ 4:03-4:36).
7. **Jerk budget.** Each week, rate three buckets high/medium/low/zero: court (basketball sessions), max jumps + sprints, fast lifts. Slow strength is exempt.
   - At most one bucket may be high.
   - Court high [inference: ≥3 hard basketball sessions/week] → jumps ≤ medium, fast lifts ≤ low (default zero).
   - `durable = false` → one bucket high, others low.
   - Never all three high.
   (PJF, mNbpZ9-7XM0 @ 2:50-5:27)
8. **Day after basketball.** If yesterday had `{basketball, hard, jumping}`, today schedules no HI-jump work and no fast lifts, unless rule 1's back-to-back is earned. Slow heavy strength is allowed (PJF, mNbpZ9-7XM0 @ 1:10, NF658w-s8io @ 12:16). [inference] The videos give no contacts-per-basketball-minute figure, so count the session as one HI-jump day, not as a contact number.
9. **Jumping inside basketball.** If court is high and `goal = jump`, take the week's max jumps as a short block in the basketball warm-up or right after pickup. Give no separate plyo day and zero fast lifts (PJF, mNbpZ9-7XM0 @ 6:46-7:09).
10. **Level gate.** Unlock plyos in order: snap-down/drop-freeze → box-jump → vertical-jump-practice → depth-jump-to-box (12-18 in) → depth-jump (24 in) → single-leg-depth-landing-hold → single-leg variations (TMS, _YudFDcW_Iw).
    - Unlock the next level after [inference] 3 logged sessions at the current level with no knee flag.
    - Hold single-leg-depth-landing-hold ≥3 weeks (TMS "a few weeks", @ 4:12).
    - Depth jumps also need ≥4 weeks of strength phase (TMS, 1CFAZfxEkoQ @ 7:12).
11. **Poor SSC profile.** If CMJ < 1.10 × SJ, or the 12- and 18-in drop jumps are ≤ CMJ, use extensive plyos only (bunny-hops, submax bounds, jump rope). Block depth jumps, hurdle hops and max bounding, and never repeat max jumps continuously (PJF, k4QD_g3nkaU @ 2:55-4:47).
12. **Cranky knee / low readiness.** On `kneeFlag` or `readiness ≤ 2`:
    - Swap every impact-high plyo for an impact-low power drill (trap-bar-jump-to-boxes, box-jump), but only if max jumping is pain-free (PJF, AR1JI0RFsbQ @ 3:36).
    - If patellar tendon pain is present, allow no max jumping at all. Use spanish-squat-isometric, single-leg-leg-extension-isometric and tempo-heavy-slow-squat (PJF, AR1JI0RFsbQ @ 4:00; 6oBTSuxggLc @ 5:00-9:55).
13. **Pre-session primer.** If `kneeHistory = true`, prepend 2-4 x 45 s spanish-squat-isometric before any basketball, plyo or lower-body strength session (PJF, 6oBTSuxggLc @ 6:24-6:40).
14. **Tendon dose.**
    - Schedule tendon isometrics or HSR (heavy slow resistance) ≥2x/week for ≥12 weeks, with 20-40 s holds at RPE 7.
    - Then drop to maintenance: 3 x 30 s, 2-3x/week.
    - Put max-force isos at the session start and yielding holds at the end.
    - Block ballistic isos when a tendon is flagged.
    (TMS, h8zKS5qZZdY @ 5:38-7:44, 9:50-10:40, 13:37, 16:12)
15. **Mechanics first.** If `approachVert - standingVert < 4 in` (15-ft approach), add band-resisted-penultimate-step plus two- and one-foot approach jumps before adding strength volume. Schedule the changes for the off-season, not mid-season (PJF, wrF0h4YadvM @ 2:12, 33:16, 43:23).
16. **Strength vs plyo ratio** (when force data exists): DSI <0.6 → lifting 1x/week and plyo priority; 0.6-0.8 → concurrent; >0.8 → strength priority (TMS citing PJF, 11uS8bwkfcc @ 10:55-11:35).
17. **Phase clock.** Run 12-week blocks:
    - Weeks 1-4: hypertrophy, full-range squats, dumbbell-loaded-jump 4x5.
    - Weeks 5-8: strength 6-8 reps, single-leg plyos.
    - Weeks 9-12: unloaded or assisted, shortest contact.
    - Then cycle back. Never run >4 weeks in phase 3 (TMS, 1CFAZfxEkoQ).
    - Within each block, shift the contact mix toward fewer, more intense contacts (TMS, zLXua2DzQ1o @ 6:35-7:22).
18. **Weekly test.** Log a vertical jump at the first session of each week, after an identical warm-up. Show progress after ~4 weeks against a "1-2 in/month" ceiling (TMS, 1CFAZfxEkoQ @ 7:27, 9:31; 11uS8bwkfcc @ 1:32).
19. **Dunk session.** Start at 10 min and progress to 20. Use the go-to takeoff for the first half and the weak side for the second. Count it as a HI-jump day and as max jumps for rule 6 (PJF, wrF0h4YadvM @ 65:11-65:42).
20. **Tendon session length.** Cap a dedicated tendon-loading session at ~10 min; extra time adds no tendon benefit (PJF citing Baar, 6oBTSuxggLc @ 2:35-3:07).
