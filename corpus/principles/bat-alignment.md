# BAT 28-Day Full Body Alignment: principles

Source: BAT Level 1 28-Day Protocol, coach Gabby (`bat-28day/EXERCISE-LIST.md`). Program: `corpus/programs/bat-28day.json`.

## Philosophy

- **Structure first, symptoms second.** Pain is a signal that tension has crossed a threshold. BAT skips massage, stretching, and foam rolling and fixes the structural position creating the tension; once structure returns toward neutral, tension and often pain drop on their own.
- **Tension first, alignment second.** A body holding protective tension can only compensate or force a position, so combined sessions release tension from the distal ends inward before alignment work.
- **Down-regulate before you train.** Floor work, slow passive movement, and long quiet breathing move the body out of sympathetic (fight or flight) into parasympathetic (recovery). A body stuck in a protective pattern cannot accept new movement.
- **Align from the base up.** Fix the Leaning Tower of Pisa from the bottom: feet and vertical axis before shoulders. Aligning lower layers exposes upper-layer compensation; new tension in new places is expected.
- **Repetition over novelty.** "Not daily increase, but daily decrease." The body adapts to repeatable, low-threat exposure. Feeling better is not owning the change: do not feel good and train heavy the next day.

## Standing rules (Week 2 onward, applied throughout)

- Slow is the method. Speed is compensation.
- No forcing range. No pushing through.
- No ribcage bracing down. No squeezing glutes to fake stability.
- Soft means quiet face and jaw, breath stays low, feet stay centred.

## Week-by-week arc

| Week | Theme | What it builds |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (Days 1-7) | Neurological Reset + Global Tension Unloading | Breath baseline, floor down-regulation, chest-pelvis breathing, lower-body fascia release |
| 2 (Days 8-14) | Vertical Axis | Foot to tibia to femur to pelvis to ribs as one stacked line; Triple Flexion & Extension is the signature movement |
| 3 (Days 15-21) | Horizontal Axis + Rib-Spine + Shoulder | Distal-to-proximal arm release, rib re-opening, scapula resting on the rib cage. The horizontal axis enters only once the vertical axis is stable |
| 4 (Days 22-28) | Total Three-Axis Integration | Foot tripod, then full-plan repeats. No new movements after Day 22; the goal is consistency, not collecting exercises |

The axes influence each other, so Day 20 onward trains them together.

## Breathing rules

- **Body Breathing is the baseline** for every movement. Fingertips on the second rib; push them away with the ribs. Structure moves first, air enters on its own. Silent, no sniffing. Only the inhale is work; the exhale is letting go. Build inhales from about 8 to 20 seconds without buying length with tension.
- Work the restricted upper, mid, and lateral ribs, not the easy lower ribs.
- No shrugging, no neck or trap tension, no abdominal bracing to exhale.
- Chest-pelvis drills train the exhale only, with a soft rounded "oh" mouth; tight lips tighten everything below. Elastic Recoil holds the sealed inhale for 3 seconds, never longer.

## Self-tests

- **Schedule:** Wall-Leaning Test and Belly Rotation Test on Days 0, 7, 21, 28. Standing photos and overhead squat videos on Days 0, 14, 28.
- **Wall-Leaning:** feet together, heels one fist from the wall, fall back without posing. Contact order should be pelvis, lumbar, thoracic, head. A lumbar gap or forced contact means extension bias; a mid-back that cannot reach means thoracic rigidity or rib flare; head first means the neck is over-leading.
- **Belly Rotation:** supine, hips 90, knees together, phone on the navel. The frame where the phone drops is the limit per side. Compare left and right.
- **Reading progress:** compare only with your own Day 0. One inch closer to the wall counts. The goal is a shrinking left-right difference, not more range; the previously better side may lose a little range as the two even out.
