# Jump science (RP / Dr. Mike Israetel)

Source: "Exercise Scientist Critiques IShowSpeed" (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n1-KuZ9aOHM. Entertainment video; only the coaching segments are used.

## Fixed (or barely trainable) factors
- Limb length: longer femurs give more time to push force into the ground before the feet leave it, so more impulse and a higher jump (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM).
- Fiber type: glutes and quads that are more fast-twitch contract faster. Twitch type is "trainable to a small extent, but it's super super genetic" (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM).
- Tendon stiffness: "this isn't huge, but it is a factor." Lax tendons dissipate muscle force as heat, stiff tendons transmit it. Mostly fixed; "some advanced stuff" exists, but it is not described (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM).
- Best-case profile: big strong quads and glutes, fast-twitch dominance, stiff tendons, low body mass with long femurs (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM).

## Trainable factors
- Quad and glute strength: "if you have big, strong quads and glutes" you jump higher. Mostly "a matter of getting as strong as possible" (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM).
- Body mass: "The less you weigh, the higher you can jump." A squat that rises while bodyweight falls means a big vertical gain (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM).
- Rate of force: more force in less time before takeoff means a higher jump. Jump practice trains contracting "everything rapidly" (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM).
- Technique: arm swing and body positioning/approach "can all be trained." Have someone review your technique so the movement chains together "less awkwardly and more athletically" (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM).
- Landing: landing is a real concern, harder in competition where you must stick it. Landing with load well above bodyweight from height raises injury risk and must be eased into (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM).

## Vertical vs broad jump
- Vertical uses a little more quad, broad uses a little more glute (pulling you laterally). The other difference is technique (arms, body position) and landing (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM).
- Good vertical power means broad jump ability is "a rounding error away," and the reverse. The movements are "insanely related" (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM).
- Deep squats and deep lunges for sets of 3-6 plus practicing the vertical jump prepare you about 90% for the broad jump, and vice versa (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM).

## Implications for programming (video's recommendations only)
- Get stronger with sets of 3-5 in squats (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM).
- Practice jumping regularly to train rapid contraction (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM).
- Get technique checked by a coach (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM).
- Keep squat strength rising while bodyweight stays low or drops (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM).
- Train deep squats and deep lunges for 3-6 reps and practice both vertical and broad jumps, since each carries over to the other (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM).
- Add landing load gradually (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM).
- [inference] Jump practice is fast, high-intensity work, so place it early in a session or on a separate day from heavy squats. The video gives no frequency or volume for jumps.
- [inference] The video gives no weekly jump dose, box heights, or tendon-stiffness drills, so the app should take those from other sources.
