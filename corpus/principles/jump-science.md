# Jump science (RP / Dr. Mike Israetel)

Source: "Exercise Scientist Critiques IShowSpeed" (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n1-KuZ9aOHM. Entertainment video; only the coaching segments are used (jump science 1:50-4:26, landing 10:06-10:32, vertical vs broad 12:43-14:43). Timestamps come from YouTube's native transcript.

## Fixed (or barely trainable) factors
- Limb length: longer femurs give more time to push force into the ground until the butt and then the feet leave it, so more impulse and a higher jump (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM @ 1:50).
- Fiber type: glutes and quads that are more fast-twitch contract faster when the nervous system says go. Twitch type is "trainable to a small extent, but it's super super genetic" (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM @ 2:17).
- Tendon stiffness: "this isn't huge, but it is a factor." Lax tendons dissipate muscle force as heat, stiff tendons transmit it. Mostly fixed; "some advanced stuff" exists, but it is not described (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM @ 2:38, 4:10).
- Best-case profile: big strong quads and glutes, fast-twitch dominance, relatively stiff tendons, low body mass with long femurs (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM @ 3:16).

## Trainable factors
- Quad and glute strength: "if you have big, strong quads and glutes" you jump higher. Mostly "a matter of getting as strong as possible" (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM @ 3:16, 4:10).
- Body mass: "The less you weigh, the higher you can jump." A squat that rises while bodyweight falls means a big vertical gain (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM @ 4:18).
- Rate of force: more force in less time before leaving the ground means a higher jump. Jump practice trains contracting "everything rapidly" (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM @ 3:06, 3:54).
- Technique: arm swing and body positioning/approach "can all be trained." Have someone review your technique so the movement chains together "less awkwardly and more athletically" (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM @ 3:16, 3:54).
- Landing: landing is a real concern, harder in competition jumps where you must actually land, not just mark the distance (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM @ 13:32). Landing from height with load well above bodyweight raises injury risk; it is "not an injury-causing thing" in itself, but you must ease into it or be very expert (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM @ 10:06).

## Vertical vs broad jump
- Vertical uses a little more quad, broad uses a little more glute (glutes pull you laterally). The other differences are technique (arms, body position) and landing (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM @ 13:08).
- Turn the vertical force vector about 45 degrees and propel yourself that way with technique, and it is basically the same thing. Good vertical power means broad jump ability is "a rounding error away," and the reverse (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM @ 14:03).
- Deep squats and deep lunges for sets of 3-6 plus practicing the vertical jump prepare you about 90% for the broad jump, and vice versa. The movements are "insanely related" (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM @ 14:35).

## Implications for programming (video's recommendations only)
- Get stronger with sets of 3-5 in things like the squat (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM @ 3:54).
- Practice jumping to train rapid contraction (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM @ 3:54).
- Get technique checked by someone (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM @ 4:02).
- Keep squat strength rising while bodyweight stays low or drops (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM @ 4:18).
- Train deep squats and deep lunges for 3-6 reps and practice the vertical jump to prepare for broad jumps, and vice versa (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM @ 14:35).
- Ease into landing loads well above bodyweight (RP, n1-KuZ9aOHM @ 10:14).
- [inference] Jump practice is fast, high-intensity work, so place it early in a session or on a separate day from heavy squats. The video gives no frequency or volume for jumps.
- [inference] The video gives no weekly jump dose, box heights, or tendon-stiffness drills, so the app should take those from other sources.

## Viewer comments (Unverified)
Viewer opinion from YouTube comments, not coaching and not checked against the transcript or any study. Likes as of the pull.
- Disputes the "twitch is super genetic" line: fiber type is more trainable than stated, citing a twin study (Bathgate et al., 2018) (44 likes, @ChrisCowley1).
- Lose fat and add lean muscle to raise vertical; one viewer reports about 6 inches more after dropping 15 lb of mostly water (1 like, @doug6723).
- Get much stronger rather than "training speed"; rate of force development is said to improve only about 10% (0 likes, @socrayes2010).
- Taller athletes benefit more from heavy lifting; vertical is said to peak around 5'8"-6'0" (0 likes, @ashyyus).
- Disputes the stiff-tendon claim: elastic tendons store energy and help running economy, and flexibility aids force through range of motion (0 likes each, @MOliva-p5q, @TeesMoneyTips); one asks whether selecting for stiff tendons raises injury rates (2 likes, @dorkwell).
- Long jump further holding weights (0 likes, @spokebloke); add neuromuscular efficiency to the factor list (0 likes, @jonathancreech85).
